Hypotheses about means

t-test

Assumptions

  • The pooled t-test assumes equal group variances. Welch’s t-test (the R default) allows unequal variances.
  • Observations are independent.
  • For exact inference, the populations are normal. With large samples, the normality assumption can often be relaxed.

Hypotheses

  • Null Hypothesis (\(H_0\)): The means of the two groups are equal.
  • Alternative Hypothesis (\(H_A\)): The means of the two groups are different.
    • Two-sided: the population means differ.
    • One-sided: one population mean is greater (or less) than the other, in the direction specified in advance.

Unpaired t-test for independent samples

Formula

Welch’s t-test for two groups: \(t = \dfrac{\bar{X}_1 - \bar{X}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}}\)

Compare one group mean with a hypothesized average: \(t = \dfrac{\bar{X}_1 - \mu}{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1}}}\)

  • \(\bar X_1, \bar X_2\) - means of 1st and 2nd samples

  • \(s_1, s_2\) - standard deviations of 1st and 2nd samples

  • \(n_1, n_2\) - number of observations in each sample

  • \(\mu\) - hypothesized average (often = 0)

Parameter of t-distribution

Degrees of freedom (df) - number of independent pieces of information

  • Pooled two-sample t-test: \(df = n_1 + n_2 - 2\).
  • One-sample t-test: \(df = n_1 - 1\).
  • Welch’s t-test: the degrees of freedom use a separate approximation.

Paired t-test for dependent samples

Used to compare two related groups (e.g., before and after treatment).

Formula

\(t = \dfrac{\bar{d}}{s_d / \sqrt{n}}\)

  • where \(\bar{d}\) is the average difference

    \(\bar{d} = \dfrac{\sum^N_{i=1}(X_{2i} - X_{1i})}{N} = \dfrac{\sum^N_{i=1} d_i}{N}\),

    • \(X_{1i}\) - \(i\)-th member of the 1st sample (before treatment)

    • \(X_{2i}\) - \(i\)-th member of the 2nd sample (after treatment)

  • and \(s_d\) is the standard deviation of the differences.

    \(s_d = \sqrt{\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^N (d_i - \bar{d})^2}{N-1}}\)

Additional assumption

  • Normal distribution of differences (\(d_i\))

Parameter of t-distribution

As two samples contain the same members, \(df\) = number of members - 1

Non-Parametric Alternatives

Non-Parametric Alternative for independent samples

If you have

  • “Strange” distribution

  • “Outliers” strongly affecting the mean value

  • Rank/ordinal data

  • Strange scale (hard to interpret)

  • It does not matter how much greater one value is than another, but it is important that it is consistently greater

  • Small sample (t-test even better for small samples)

then you can use non-parametric approaches

Mann-Whitney test (also known as Wilcoxon rank-sum test)

Hypotheses

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be independent draws from the two groups. In the lecture’s location-shift setting, the distributions have the same shape.

  • \(H_0\): Neither group tends to produce higher values (stochastic equality), \(P(X>Y) = P(Y>X)\).
  • \(H_A\) (two-sided): One group tends to produce higher values, \(P(X>Y) \ne P(Y>X)\).

In this setting, \(H_0\) means zero shift and identical distributions. Counting each tie as half a win, the null win probability is \(0.5\).

Example

sample1 <- c(rnorm(20, 5, 2), 18)
sample2 <- c(rnorm(20, 7, 2), 22)

data_sample <- data.frame(group = rep(c("sample1", "sample2"), each=21),
                         value = c(sample1, sample2))
data_sample %>%  
  ggplot(aes(x = value, fill=group)) + 
  geom_density(alpha=0.5) +
  theme_classic()
t.test(value ~ group,
       data=data_sample)

    Welch Two Sample t-test

Non-Parametric Alternative for dependent samples

Wilcoxon signed-rank test

Hypotheses

The signed-rank test ranks the absolute within-pair differences and uses their signs.

  • \(H_0\): the distribution of paired differences is symmetric around zero.
  • \(H_A\) (two-sided, under a symmetric shift model): the centre of that distribution differs from zero.

Testing before/after data

                         value = c(sample_before, sample_after))

wilcox.test(sample_after, sample_before, paired = TRUE)

Categorical data testing

Chi-Square (\(\chi^2\)) Test (of independence)

Used to test relationships between categorical variables.

Assumptions

  • Observations are independent.
  • Expected cell counts are large enough for the chi-square approximation.

Hypotheses

  • Null Hypothesis (\(H_0\)): There is no association between two categorical variables
  • Alternative Hypothesis (\(H_A\)): There is some association between two categorical variables

Formula

\(\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O_i - E_i)^2}{E_i}\) where (\(O_i\)) are the observed frequencies, and (\(E_i\)) are the expected frequencies.

Parameter of \(\chi^2\)-distribution

Degrees of freedom \(df\)

  • (number of rows in contingency table - 1) * (number of columns in contingency table - 1)

Contingency tables

Does disease outcome depend on treatment group?

Cured Not cured Row Total
Treatment 12 8 20
Placebo 6 10 16
Column Total 18 18 36

Expected values calculation

In a 2x2 contingency table, the expected frequency for each cell is calculated as:

\[E_{ij} = \frac{(total \ Row_i \times total \ Column_j)}{N}\]

Where:

  • \(total \ Row_i\) = Sum of row \(i\)

  • \(total \ Column_j\) = Sum of column \(j\)

  • \(N\) = Grand total

The expected counts preserve the row and column totals and reflect independence.

Cured Not cured Row Total
Treatment \((18 \times 20)/36 = 10\) \((18 \times 20)/36 = 10\) 20
Placebo \((18 \times 16)/36 = 8\) \((18 \times 16)/36 = 8\) 16
Column Total 18 18 36

\(\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O_i - E_i)^2}{E_i}\) where (\(O_i\)) are the observed frequencies, and (\(E_i\)) are the expected

Paired categorical data testing (for dependent samples)

McNemar’s test

For example, comparing two COVID tests. Both tests either detect the virus or not in the same patients.

Assumptions

  • Patients are independent, with paired results within each patient. The chi-square approximation needs enough discordant pairs.

Hypotheses

  • \(H_0\): the two tests have equal probabilities of a positive result. Equivalently, the two types of discordant pair (cells \(b\) and \(c\)) have equal probabilities.
  • \(H_A\): the probabilities of a positive result differ.
Test2: + Test2: - Row Total
Test1: + a c a+c
Test1: - b d b+d
Column Total a+b c+d a+b+c+d

Without continuity correction, \(\chi^2 = \dfrac{(b-c)^2}{b+c}\).

Fisher’s Exact Test

Assumptions

Fisher’s test conditions on the observed row and column totals. In the tea example, four of eight cups are tea-first, and the expert must also label exactly four cups tea-first.

Actual order Expert: Tea first Expert: Milk first
Tea first 3 1
Milk first 1 3

Often used for 2×2 contingency tables.

The test is exact because it calculates probabilities under \(H_0\) conditional on those totals.

Hypotheses

  • Null Hypothesis (\(H_0\)): There is no association between two categorical variables
    • Odds ratio (OR) = 1
  • Alternative Hypothesis (\(H_A\)): There is some association between two categorical variables
    • Odds ratio (OR) \(\neq\) 1
